Everything Sono does,
and where it keeps it.

Sono is deliberately small. This page is most of what there is to know, including the two things the app cannot tell you itself: that the enhancement pass ships switched off, and that dragging the app to the Trash leaves the speech model behind.

Install

Sono needs macOS 26 or later. It is signed with a Developer ID and notarised by Apple, so it opens without any Gatekeeper warning and without right click and Open.

  1. Download the disk image and open it.
  2. Drag Sono to Applications. Running it from the mounted image works, but the permissions you grant will not carry over once you move it.
  3. Open Sono. It asks for two permissions, then downloads the speech model.

The first launch downloads about 465 MB and unpacks to roughly 640 MB. Sono checks for 1.25 GB free before it starts. The model is not in the app bundle on purpose: bundling it would make every future update a 640 MB download. It is fetched once, verified against a pinned SHA-256, and never downloaded again.

Permissions

Both are requested at first launch, together, so nothing interrupts you mid sentence later.

Microphone

To hear you. Without it Sono cannot record at all.

System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone

Accessibility

To press Command V for you in the app you are typing in. macOS treats synthetic keystrokes as a privileged action, which is why this one asks for your password.

System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility

If you decline Accessibility, Sono keeps working but stops pasting. Your text is copied to the clipboard instead and you press Command V yourself. This is easy to miss, because nothing looks broken. If you find yourself pasting manually every time, that is the reason.

Sono never asks for Speech Recognition. That permission covers Apple's dictation service, which Sono does not use. Transcription happens in Sono's own process.

Using it

The Option key does both jobs, and Sono tells them apart by how long you hold it.

GestureWhat happensGood for
Tap ⌥ Starts recording. Tap again to stop. Anything longer than a sentence, where holding a key gets tiring.
Hold ⌥ Records while held, past about a third of a second, and stops the moment you let go. A quick sentence, push to talk.

When you stop, the text lands in whatever app was focused, all at once. Sono does not type character by character. Silence at either end is trimmed before transcription, so pauses cost you nothing.

Your clipboard is put back the way it was afterwards, images and formatting included. If you copied something else in the meantime, Sono notices and leaves your newer clipboard alone.

Enhancement

This is the part that makes Sono more than a transcriber, and it is switched off when you install. That is deliberate: the first dictation you ever run should be as fast as your Mac can manage, and the cleanup adds a beat.

Turn it on in Settings → Enhancement. Both models run on this Mac. Nothing is uploaded either way.

What it fixes

  • Fillers. The ums, uhs and false starts go.
  • Self corrections. Say "on Tuesday, no, on Thursday" and you get "on Thursday".
  • Grammar and punctuation, without rewriting your voice.
  • Spoken lists become real lists, numbered and on their own lines.

Two models to choose from

OptionCostsWhat it is
OffInstant The default. Raw transcription, exactly as spoken.
Apple IntelligenceAbout a second The model already on your Mac. Nothing to download, but it needs a Mac that supports Apple Intelligence, and its context window is 4,096 tokens.
Local model2 to 2.5 seconds Qwen3 4B, a one time 2.3 GB download. Runs on any Apple Silicon Mac, including where Apple Intelligence is unavailable. 32,768 token context, so long dictations are not cut short.

The local model is the more thorough of the two on lists and self corrections, and it is the slower of the two. Settings shows all three options with what each one costs you.

When it declines

Sono never trusts the language model blindly, whichever one you pick. The polished text is thrown away and your raw transcript is used instead if the result comes back empty, more than about twice as long as the original (the model answered the transcript instead of editing it), less than half as long (it ate a clause), or opens like a refusal.

A bad model response therefore costs you polish, never words. On a Mac without Apple Intelligence available, that option is greyed out and the local model is the one to use.

Your data

Where it lives

WhatWhere
Transcript history ~/Library/Application Support/Sono/history.jsonl
Speech model ~/Library/Application Support/Sono/models/
Preferences Standard macOS preferences for com.aeyar.Sono

The history file is newline delimited JSON, one dictation per line, so you can read it, grep it, or process it with anything. Nothing is encrypted, because nothing is hidden from you.

What the dashboard counts

Every figure is derived from that one history file. There is no separate analytics store, and nothing is sent anywhere. It tracks dictations, words and characters, total time spoken, a daily words chart, and a per app breakdown showing which apps you dictate into most.

What leaves your Mac

Two requests, and neither carries audio or text. The one time model download on first launch, and a daily check for a new version, which sends a version number and nothing else. System profiling is disabled, so no macOS version, CPU or Mac model is attached. You can turn the update check off in Settings.

Removing everything

Dragging Sono to the Trash removes only the app. The 640 MB model and your entire transcript history stay on disk. For an app that promises your words stay on your machine, leaving them behind is the wrong default.

Use Settings → Remove all data instead. It tells you exactly how much it is about to delete, removes the model, the history wherever you moved it to, and the preferences, then quits. Drag the app itself to the Trash afterwards; a running app cannot delete its own bundle.

Troubleshooting

Text goes to the clipboard instead of being pasted

Accessibility is not granted. System Settings → Privacy & Security → Accessibility, and switch Sono on. If Sono is already listed, switch it off and on again; a stale entry pointing at an older copy of the app will look correct but grant nothing.

The first launch sits on "Downloading model"

It is fetching about 465 MB. On a slow connection this takes a while. It happens once. If it fails, Sono verifies the download against a checksum and will fetch it again rather than run on a corrupted model.

Nothing happens when I hold Option

Hold it a little longer. Push to talk starts after about a third of a second, so that ordinary use of Option as a modifier does not start a recording. A quick tap toggles recording instead.

The text is accurate but still has my ums in it

That is the raw transcript, which means the enhancement pass is off. It ships that way. Settings → Enhancement.

Enhancement is on but nothing seems different

Sono discards the polished text and keeps your words whenever the result looks wrong, so a bad response is invisible by design. It also needs a Mac with Apple Intelligence available. Shorter dictations polish more reliably than very long ones.

Something else

Open an issue on GitHub, or email support@heysono.app. Sono is open source, so the answer to "what does it actually do here" is always readable.
